My Favourite Studio Ghibli Films

Studio Ghibli has produced a wide range of films, each with its own tone, themes, and emotional impact. Below are some of my personal favourites — films that have stayed with me long after watching them, not just because of their stories, but because of how they feel.

Spirited Away (2001)

Director: Hayao Miyazaki

My Rating: ★★★★★

Spirited Away follows Chihiro, a young girl who becomes trapped in a mysterious spirit world after her parents are transformed into pigs. What begins as a surreal and overwhelming journey gradually becomes a story about growth, resilience, and identity.

What makes this film unforgettable for me is its atmosphere. The bathhouse, the spirits, and the quiet train scene all feel dreamlike yet strangely familiar. It’s a film that doesn’t explain everything — and that’s exactly why it stays with you.

Howl’s Moving Castle (2004)

Director: Hayao Miyazaki

My Rating: ★★★★★

This film tells the story of Sophie, a young woman cursed into an old body, and her encounter with the mysterious wizard Howl. Set against the backdrop of a moving castle and a war-torn world, the film blends romance, fantasy, and anti-war themes.

I love how chaotic yet alive everything feels — especially the castle itself. Despite its fantastical elements, the film is deeply human, focusing on insecurity, self-worth, and emotional growth.

Kiki’s Delivery Service (1989)

Director: Hayao Miyazaki

My Rating: ★★★★☆

Kiki’s Delivery Service follows a young witch who moves to a new city to become independent. Unlike many fantasy films, the story is quiet and grounded, focusing on everyday struggles like burnout, loneliness, and finding purpose.

What stands out most is how relatable it feels. Kiki’s journey isn’t about saving the world — it’s about figuring out who she is. That simplicity makes it incredibly powerful.

Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind (1984)

Director: Hayao Miyazaki

My Rating: ★★★★★

Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind is set in a post-apocalyptic world where humanity struggles to survive alongside a toxic jungle filled with giant insects. Nausicaä, the princess of a small valley, seeks to understand the natural world rather than fight against it.

What makes this film stand out is its message. Instead of framing nature as something to conquer, it emphasizes coexistence, empathy, and understanding. Nausicaä herself is a deeply compassionate character, driven not by power, but by her desire to protect both people and the environment. The film feels grand in scale, yet remains emotionally grounded through her perspective.
